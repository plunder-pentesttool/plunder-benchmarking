============================================================
PLUNDER BENCHMARK REPORT
============================================================

1. OBJECTIVE
------------------------------------------------------------

Plunder was evaluated on a controlled pentesting dataset
containing configuration and environment files with known
cleartext credentials.

The objective was to measure Plunder's ability to identify
known leaked passwords while minimizing false positives.


2. BENCHMARK DATASET
------------------------------------------------------------

    /benchmarking

cleartext credentials:

3. PLUNDER CONFIGURATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Entropy metric:
    [Negative Log Likelihood]

Outlier detection:
    DBSCAN

DBSCAN parameters:
    eps = [1.5]
    min_samples = [7]

Minimum candidate word length:
    4 characters

REGEX / HEURISTIC FILTERING:
    Candidates are restricted to 6–32 characters and must contain only
    alphanumeric characters and !@#$%^&*?.

    Candidates must contain:
    (letters + digits) OR (letters + symbols)

    Tokens beginning with a symbol are rejected. Scanning is also limited
    to selected configuration, source-code, credential, and deployment
    file types.

Maximum file size:
    [200 KB]

6. INTERPRETATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Plunder detected [8] of the 14 known cleartext
credentials, corresponding to a recall of [57.14]%.

TruffleHog detected [0] of the 14 known credentials,
with a recall of [0.00]% and precision of [0.00]%.

Compared with TruffleHog, Plunder showed [57.14]
higher recall and [33.33] higher precision.

Plunder's main advantage was ability to
identify previously unknown high-information strings with
no dependence on application and vendor specific regular
expression patterns.


7. LIMITATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

- The benchmark dataset is relatively small.
- The passwords were intentionally planted and therefore do
  not represent the full diversity of real-world credentials.
- Results depend on the selected entropy and DBSCAN parameters.
- The benchmark does not measure detection of every type of
  secret supported by established secret-scanning tools.
- Additional evaluation on larger real-world or publicly
  available datasets is required.
